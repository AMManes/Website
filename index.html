California · 2016 to 2025

Farmland Acreage Change

The project compares California farmland classifications over time. The reported combined acreage increased by 230,339 acres, while the mix shifted: local-importance farmland gained acreage as prime and state-importance farmland declined.

Reported category values
Classification2016 acres2025 acresChange
Prime farmland5,031,4774,958,493-72,984
Semi-agricultural49,84552,300+2,455
State importance2,544,4782,496,148-48,330
Local importance2,852,8363,185,352+332,516
Unique farmlandNot statedNot stated+16,682

The category totals show why acreage alone is not the whole story. A net gain does not by itself establish improved soil health, sustainability, or a cause for the classification changes.

Los Angeles · Police-station proximity

Crime and Buffer Analysis

This ArcGIS Pro study asks whether neighborhood crime rates differ inside one-mile police-station buffers compared with areas farther away. Crime counts were normalized by population, then spatially joined to buffer areas for comparison.

Question and hypotheses

Research question: Are crime rates lower in Los Angeles neighborhoods within one mile of a police station?

Null: Mean crime rates are similar inside and outside the one-mile buffers. Alternative: Rates are lower inside the buffers.

Reported statistical result

The project reports t = -1.677 and p = 0.0502. For the stated one-tailed test at alpha = 0.10, the reported p-value is below the threshold and the null hypothesis is rejected.

This is evidence of a difference under the selected test, not proof that proximity causes lower crime. The result should be interpreted alongside the study design, time period, data quality, population normalization, and other neighborhood factors.

Florida · Census tracts

Age, Income, and Spatial Hotspots

A bivariate map compares median age by sex with household income across Florida census tracts. The accompanying hotspot interpretation notes coastal clusters in median age and higher-income concentrations around the Jacksonville to Orlando corridor. These are mapped spatial patterns, not evidence that location alone causes income or age differences.

A practical workflow

Good work starts with a good question.

Every project is different. I focus on understanding the need first, then matching the right tools and point of view to the task.

01 / DEFINE

Understand the objective

Clarify the question, the audience, and what a useful result should make possible.

02 / COLLECT

Capture the right information

Plan the fieldwork and data collection around the intended use, whether from the air, existing datasets, or both.

03 / INTERPRET

Find the signal

Bring spatial analysis and careful review to the data, keeping the context and limitations in view.

04 / DELIVER

Make the result clear

Shape findings into maps, imagery, or visual design that communicates what matters.
